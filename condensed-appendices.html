B4.Counts and other numbers

  • Bones: 206 in the adult; 32 adult teeth, 20 deciduous.
  • Cervical 7, thoracic 12, lumbar 5, sacral 5 (fused), coccygeal 3–5.
  • Diaphragm motor supply: phrenic nerve C3–C5 (C4 is the key segment).
  • Intercostal vein–artery–nerve order: VAN from above downward in the costal groove.
  • Carpal tunnel: 9 tendons (4 FDS, 4 FDP, 1 FPL) + median nerve.
  • Guyon tunnel: ulnar artery + ulnar nerve (superficial and deep branches).
  • Six dorsal tendon compartments of the wrist; six extensor compartments.
  • Rotator cuff: 4 muscles (supraspinatus, infraspinatus, teres minor, subscapularis) — SITS.
  • Spinohumeral and thoracohumeral groups: a departmental teaching convention (muscles connecting the axial skeleton to the shoulder apparatus) — always define the grouping first, then give the attachments; see Topic I.11.
  • Bronchopulmonary segments: 10 per lung (right and left, with minor variations).
  • Lung: right 3 lobes / 2 fissures, left 2 lobes / 1 fissure (by the aortic impression).
  • Thyroid cartilage C4–C5; cricoid C6; the trachea begins at C6 below the cricoid (cricotracheal ligament), so the first tracheal cartilage lies inferior to the cricoid.
  • Trachea length 10–12 cm, diameter 1.5–2 cm; bifurcates at T4–T5 (sternal angle).
  • Total lung capacity ~6 L; tidal volume ~500 mL.
  • Red blood cells ~120 days; platelets 8–10 days; haemoglobin 13–17 g/dL (sex dependent).
  • Thymus: greatest relative size at puberty, then involutes; bilobed, in the superior mediastinum.
  • Spleen: about 12 cm × 7 cm × 4 cm, 150–200 g; ribs 9–11.
  • Thoracic duct: begins at the cisterna chyli (L1–L2), opens at the left venous angle.
  • Tracheal bifurcation, aortic arch and azygos arch are all at the sternal angle (T4/T5).

Appendix C — Radiology: how to describe a film, and what to look for

C1.The five-step answer for any image

  1. Projection and quality — what view is it (AP, PA, lateral, mortise, skyline, axial), is it adequately penetrated and centred, are the right structures included.
  2. Bones and alignment — cortical outline, trabecular pattern, joint congruity, lines (see C2), growth plates.
  3. Joint space and cartilage — width, symmetry, osteophytes, subchondral sclerosis/cysts, erosions.
  4. Soft tissues — muscle planes, fat pads/stripes (abnormal when displaced, obliterated or containing fluid), calcification, gas.
  5. Conclusion — the single most likely diagnosis plus one differential.

C3.Ossification timetable (the classic oral question)

StageWhat appears
Intra-uterine weeks 5–8Primary ossification centres appear in the diaphyses, in a general cranio-caudal direction; the clavicle is the first bone to ossify (membranous), the femur and humerus follow
At birthSecondary centres present in the distal femur and proximal tibia (the only two regularly present, and therefore used for fetal maturity); the calcaneus also has one
Elbow, “CRITOE”Capitellum (~1 y) → Radial head (3–5 y) → Internal (medial) epicondyle (5–7 y) → Trochlea (7–9 y) → Olecranon (9–11 y) → External (lateral) epicondyle (11–13 y)
CarpusCapitate first, then hamate, triquetrum, lunate, scaphoid, trapezium, trapezoid; pisiform last (about 9–12 y)
ClosureEpiphyseal plates close progressively; the proximal humerus and distal radius are among the last (about 18–20 y). Girls mature about 2 years earlier than boys.

Appendix E — General embryology timeline

TimeEvents worth naming in the exam
Day 0–1Fertilisation in the ampullary part of the uterine tube; second meiotic division completed; zygote formed; the cortico-medullary reaction avoids polyspermy
Day 1–3Cleavage: 2-, 4-, 8-cell stages; morula by day 3–4 (still within the zona pellucida)
Day 4–5Blastocyst: trophoblast, inner cell mass (embryoblast) and blastocoele; zona pellucida disappears, so implantation can occur
Day 6–7Attachment to the endometrium; trophoblast differentiates into cytotrophoblast and syncytiotrophoblast; the hypoblast and epiblast form the bilaminar disc
Week 2Implantation completed; amniotic cavity and yolk sac form; extraembryonic mesoderm; primary chorionic villi
Week 3Gastrulation: primitive streak, then the three germ layers; notochord and prechordal plate; neural plate appears; secondary villi and tertiary villi with fetal vessels
Week 4Neurulation: neural tube closes (anterior neuropore day 25, posterior day 27); neural crest migrates; somites, intermediate mesoderm and lateral plate mesoderm differentiate; the heart begins to beat around day 22
Weeks 5–8Organogenesis; limb buds and segmentation; facial and pharyngeal arch development; the embryo becomes recognisably human by week 8
Weeks 9–38Fetal period: growth and differentiation of organs; the placenta reaches its mature form; vernix caseosa and lanugo appear in the later fetal period
Placenta
VilliPrimary villi (week 2, trophoblast columns) → secondary villi (week 3, with extraembryonic mesodermal core) → tertiary villi (week 3–4, with fetal capillaries)
Placental barrierEarly pregnancy: syncytiotrophoblast → cytotrophoblast → villous connective tissue → fetal capillary endothelium. Late pregnancy the cytotrophoblast becomes discontinuous, so the barrier is thinner and antibody transfer (IgG) increases
CirculationTwo umbilical arteries (branches of the internal iliac arteries) and one umbilical vein (carrying oxygenated blood to the ductus venosus); maternal blood in the intervillous space

Final revision checklist
  • Can you draw, from memory, the brachial plexus and the lumbosacral plexus with root values?
  • Can you name every muscle in the four rotator-cuff + six spinohumeral + three thoracohumeral groups with innervation?
  • Can you list the contents of the carpal tunnel, Guyon tunnel, femoral canal, adductor canal and popliteal fossa?
  • Can you give the vertebral level of every diaphragmatic opening and the root value of the phrenic nerve?
  • Can you describe the layers of the thoracic wall and the order of the neurovascular bundle?
  • Can you name the 10 bronchopulmonary segments on each side?
  • Can you identify all 48 histology slides from two features each (Appendix D)?
  • Can you state the stage of development at day 8, week 3, week 4 and week 8?
